# Small landlords and maintenance: market notes (sample data)

*Illustrative figures written for the Aktor Studies walkthrough. They are plausible, not sourced:
replace them with your own research before relying on a verdict.*

## Who they are

- About **48,000 independent landlords** in the target metro areas own between 5 and 50 units.
  Most manage their buildings themselves or with one part-time helper.
- Roughly **30%** already use property management software (mainly for rent collection and
  listings); fewer than **5%** use anything for maintenance beyond a shared inbox or spreadsheet.

## The problem

- A landlord with 20 units gets **8 to 15 maintenance requests a month**, mostly by text or phone,
  often at night. About one in five is urgent (water, heat, locks).
- In a survey of 220 small landlords, **61%** named "sorting and dispatching repairs" among their
  three most time-consuming tasks; the median time spent was **6 hours a month**.
- **38%** said they had lost a tenant in the past two years partly because of slow repairs.

## What they pay for today

- Rent-collection and listing tools: **$15–$45 a month** per landlord at this size.
- Full-service property managers charge **8–10% of rent**, which most small landlords avoid.
- Asked what they would pay for a tool that triages requests and books a vetted contractor,
  survey respondents answered: under $20 (34%), $20–$40 (41%), $40–$60 (18%), over $60 (7%).

## Competition

- Two general property-management suites offer a maintenance module, but only on plans that start
  at **$150 a month**, aimed at portfolios of 100+ units.
- One start-up offers AI triage for tenants of large operators; it doesn't sell to small landlords.

## Reaching them

- Landlord associations and online forums are the main channels. Paid search for
  "landlord maintenance software" costs **$4–$7 per click**; comparable tools report
  **2–4%** of clicks turning into paying customers.
- Software for small landlords typically loses **3–5% of customers a month**.
